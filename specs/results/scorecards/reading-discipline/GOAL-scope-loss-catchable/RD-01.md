**The check does not distinguish a quotation from an assertion, and it must

## 4. The 8 it cannot reach, counted separately and never omitted

| reason | n | sites |
|---|---|---|
| anaphoric scope (*"about this example"*) | 3 | `INSTRUMENT-LOG.toml:2144`, `HISTORY-ab_quota_ledger.md:281`, `complexity_ledger.json:18284` |
| arm-scoped (*"for `T`/`Q`/`X`"*, *"for arm C"*) | 3 | `FI-06.md:136`, `falsifiable-instruments/RESULTS.md:326`, `ADVERSARIAL.md:218` |
| a qualifier the corpus does not define (*"greenfield cards"*) | 2 | `SELF-IMPROVEMENT.md:1501`, `subtract-to-measure-sm05/RESULT.md:73` |

An anaphor **is** a scope; it is not one this can resolve. Arm labels are
round-local and opaque by design — `scaffold` draws them from a pool that
excludes every label a prior round published — so they cannot be resolved to a
card set at all. Neither is a claim that holds.

### And what it does not count, because it is not looking

**A figure with no count in it escapes entirely.** *"D3 is the one dimension that
discriminates and holds still"* is one of the three instances that opened this
epic and it has no `N of M`, so the sweep finds zero of that class — not because
there are none, but because the grammar is `D<n> = k on N of M`. Same for
*"every subject this project has ever scored was greenfield"*. Declared as
`scorecard-scope`'s blind spot in `examples/validation/instruments/`, filed as
`RD-01-DF-01`, and left for RD-03 rather than repaired by a parser that would
have to guess at the population behind a sentence.

**And a figure broken across a line break escapes.** Matching is line-based, so
`D1 = 3 on 23 of` / `27 cards` reads as no figure at all. Two of section 7's own
quotations wrap that way and are not in the 44 — measured, not assumed. The
limit is kept rather than removed: widening the window to a paragraph is what
lets a figure bind to a scope three sentences away, which is the defect.

## 7. What was REJECTED, and every one of them would have lowered the count

Five designs were built far enough to run and then thrown away. Three of them
produced a smaller, more comfortable number.

**A "this is only a quotation" marker.** The nine propagation sites are
quotations, and a marker excusing them would have cut the refuted count from 13
to 4. **Rejected as a suppression key.** Any unflattering site could be dismissed
by putting quote marks round it, and this project has already shipped one
construct — `EVAL-SUPPRESS` — that erased a demonstrated kill with a declaration.
What replaced it is not a marker but a mechanical test with teeth: is the scope
*beside* the figure? Name the example there and the claim gets evaluated against
that example's cards, where a false one is still refuted.

**Resolving scope against epic slugs as well as example ids.** It ran, and it
evaluated `NEXT-EPIC.md:30` and `ticket_plan.yaml:117` against **12** cards
instead of 49, cutting their counterexamples from 8 to 4 — for the wrong reason.
Both sentences contain the string `subtract-to-measure` because that is the epic
that *made* the claim, not the population it is *about*. It would also have
scoped away any figure that happens to mention the round it came from.
**Rejected: a smaller number reached by misreading a sentence is worse than a
large one, and the misreading here is exactly the epic's subject.**

**Requiring the counted noun to be card-like.** Tempting, because it silences
`| **D1** | worst 1, 2 of 6 moved |` cleanly. It also silences
`falsifiable-instruments/RESULTS.md:344` — *"Constant at 2 on 27 of 27"*, which
has no noun at all and is one of the four live assertions — and five more. It
would have cut the count by six. **Rejected.** The discrimination that survived
is different and does not cost those six: the count has to be introduced as a
*scope* (`on`, `for`, `in`, `across`), which is what separates "D1 = 3 on 23 of
27 cards" from "Moved 0 of 40 against EVAL-RERUN" in the same table cell.

**Treating every off-value card as a counterexample, including on partial
counts.** This one would have *raised* the number, and it was rejected too. Where
a claim says `23 of 27` the other four are the remainder it already accounts for,
and calling them counterexamples is the check making the claim say more than it
does. Partial counts are re-derived as a ratio instead, and `denominator_rule`
says which of the two terms moved.

**One more, rejected for a different reason: making `scope` a close gate.** It
was two lines. Five epics of static checking on the product caught zero bugs, and
a check that has to be satisfied before a workflow closes is a check somebody
will satisfy. `scope` exits 1 on this repository as shipped and nothing consults
it — it is a reading of the record, and the reading is the product.
